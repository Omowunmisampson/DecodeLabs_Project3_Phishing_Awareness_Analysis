# Phishing Triage Toolkit

## 1. Phishing Red Flag Checklist

### Sender Check
- Does the sender's name match the email address?
- Is the email domain familiar and legitimate?
- Is the sender impersonating an organization or executive?

### Link and Domain Check
- Does the link lead to the expected website?
- Is the domain misspelled or designed to look legitimate?
- Is the URL unusual or suspicious?

### Urgency Check
- Does the message pressure the recipient to act immediately?
- Does it threaten account closure or payment failure?
- Is it creating fear or panic?

### Request Check
- Is the message requesting passwords, OTPs, payment details, or sensitive information?
- Is it asking the recipient to bypass normal procedures?

### Attachment Check
- Is the attachment unexpected?
- Is the file type unusual or potentially dangerous?

### Other Warning Signs
- Unexpected QR codes
- Unfamiliar phone numbers
- Unexpected MFA approval requests
- Fake forwarded messages
- Unusual requests from colleagues or executives

## 2. Phishing Triage Decision Tree

### Step 1: Receive the Message

Treat unexpected or unusual messages with caution.

### Step 2: Check for Red Flags

Review the sender, domain, links, attachments, urgency, and requests for sensitive information.

### Step 3: Classify the Message

#### SAFE
No obvious warning signs are identified based on the available information.

**Action:** Follow normal organizational procedures and remain cautious.

#### SUSPICIOUS
One or more warning signs are present, but there is not enough evidence to confirm malicious activity.

**Action:**
- Do not click links.
- Do not open attachments.
- Verify through a trusted, separate channel.
- Report the message if necessary.

#### MALICIOUS
Several strong indicators suggest that the message is likely a phishing or social engineering attempt.

**Action:**
- Do not interact with the message.
- Do not provide information or make payments.
- Report and escalate according to organizational procedures.

## 3. Golden Rule

**PAUSE → VERIFY → REPORT**

- **PAUSE:** Stop before clicking, replying, calling, scanning, or approving anything.
- **VERIFY:** Confirm the request through a trusted channel separate from the suspicious message.
- **REPORT:** Report the suspicious message to the appropriate security team or reporting channel.

## 4. Educational Examples

The toolkit was tested with fictional examples involving:

- A normal project update
- A suspicious account-lockout message
- A fraudulent executive transfer request

The examples demonstrate how urgency, impersonation, suspicious domains, and requests to bypass procedures can indicate phishing or social engineering.

## Disclaimer

The examples are fictional and educational. Real phishing investigations may require additional technical checks, email-header analysis, and security tools.
